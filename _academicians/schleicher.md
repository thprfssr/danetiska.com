**August Schleicher** (1821–1868) was a German linguist whose work helped establish the reconstruction of Proto-Indo-European as a central undertaking of comparative linguistics. He studied the relationships among the Indo-European languages and sought to describe the earlier forms from which their recorded grammars had developed.

His *Compendium of the Comparative Grammar of the Indo-European Languages* brought together a substantial body of comparative knowledge. He also used branching family trees to represent linguistic descent.

In 1868, Schleicher published a short fable about a sheep and horses in reconstructed Indo-European. Its language has since been revised many times as reconstruction has advanced. The attempt remains memorable: a grammar assembled from comparisons was made to tell a story.

The Danetian Academy honors Schleicher for helping scholars ask what an unrecorded language might have been like. For a language built with an Indo-European past, his work belongs close to home.
