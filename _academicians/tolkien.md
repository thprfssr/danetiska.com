**John Ronald Reuel Tolkien** (3 January 1892 – 2 September 1973) was an
English philologist, writer, and creator of languages. Born in Bloemfontein
in the Orange Free State, now part of South Africa, he grew up in England
and pursued an academic career devoted to early English language and
literature. He taught for much of his life at the University of Oxford.

Tolkien is best known for *The Hobbit* (1937) and *The Lord of the Rings*
(1954–1955). Alongside his scholarship and fiction, he developed invented
languages, alphabets, and the histories of the peoples who used them.

## Language and imagined history

Language creation occupied a central place in Tolkien’s imaginative work.
Quenya and Sindarin, his principal Elvish languages, were conceived with
distinct sounds, grammatical structures, and histories of development.
Their relationships reflect the historical processes studied by philologists:
common ancestry, divergence, and change over time.

For Tolkien, languages and mythology grew together. His imagined languages
belonged to peoples with memories, traditions, and literary cultures; the
stories gave those languages a setting, while the languages helped shape
the stories.

## Honorary recognition

The Danetian Academy honors Tolkien for the care and imagination he brought to
making languages. He gave them histories, poetry, and people to speak them. For
those of us who find joy in creating a language and discovering the world around
it, his work is a source of inspiration and a reminder that this is a pursuit
worth taking seriously.
