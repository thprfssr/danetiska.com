**Francesco Petrarca** (1304–1374), known in English as **Petrarch**, was an Italian poet and scholar born in Arezzo. His writing helped shape Renaissance humanism and gave European lyric poetry one of its most enduring models.

His *Canzoniere* brings together 366 poems in Italian, many addressed to or concerned with Laura. Love, memory, grief, and the passage of time recur throughout the collection. Petrarch returned to these poems over many years, revising the words through which later generations would come to know him.

He also wrote extensively in Latin and searched for manuscripts of ancient authors. For Petrarch, reading the classics could feel like a conversation across centuries. His letters and scholarly interests helped renew attention to the lives, language, and writings of the ancient world.

The Danetian Academy honors Petrarch as a poet and a devoted reader. His care for individual lines, his affection for old books, and his willingness to return to unfinished work offer much to anyone trying to build a literature of their own.
