**Franz Bopp** (1791–1867) was a German linguist and one of the founders of comparative Indo-European grammar. His early study of Sanskrit led him to examine its verbal system alongside those of Greek, Latin, Persian, and Germanic languages.

His study of conjugation, published in 1816, helped move the discussion of linguistic relationship toward detailed comparison. Shared ancestry could be investigated through the workings of grammar: the forms of verbs, their endings, and the patterns that connected them across languages.

Bopp developed this undertaking in his extensive comparative grammar. Although later scholarship revised many of his explanations, the scale and care of his comparisons helped establish a discipline in which claims about language history had to answer to grammatical evidence.

The Danetian Academy honors Bopp for taking inflection seriously. For those who spend their time with declensions and conjugations, his work is a reminder that even a small ending can preserve a long history.
