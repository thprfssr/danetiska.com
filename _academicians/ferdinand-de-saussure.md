**Ferdinand de Saussure** (1857–1913) was a Swiss linguist whose work helped shape both Indo-European scholarship and the study of language as a system. Before the lectures that made him widely known in general linguistics, he produced a remarkable study of the Indo-European vowel system.

His *Mémoire sur le système primitif des voyelles dans les langues indo-européennes* appeared in 1878 with an 1879 title-page date. Still a young scholar, Saussure proposed otherwise unattested elements to account for patterns in the reconstructed vowel system. His analysis became a foundation for the later development of laryngeal theory.

The subsequent study of Hittite supplied evidence that gave new force to this approach. Saussure’s proposal had shown how relationships within a system could point toward something no surviving language then known had directly revealed.

The Danetian Academy honors Saussure for that combination of close observation and bold inference. His early work remains especially welcome to anyone who has spent an afternoon trying to make sense of an ablaut pattern.
