**Sir William Jones** (1746–1794) was a British scholar, translator, and judge. His interests embraced languages, literature, law, and history. While serving in Calcutta, he helped establish the Asiatic Society and studied Sanskrit with Indian scholars.

In a discourse delivered in 1786, Jones argued that the similarities between Sanskrit, Greek, and Latin, in both vocabulary and grammatical structure, were too substantial to be accidental. He proposed that they had developed from an earlier common source, and suggested connections with other languages now recognized as Indo-European.

He was not the first person to notice relationships among these languages. His formulation nevertheless became an influential statement of the question that comparative linguists would spend the following century investigating.

The Danetian Academy honors Jones for helping give that question a clear and lasting form. Behind the different words and endings of familiar languages, he invited his readers to imagine an older language whose speakers had left no surviving account of it.
