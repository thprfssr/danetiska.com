**Leonhard Euler** (1707–1783) was a Swiss mathematician who spent much of his working life at the academies of Saint Petersburg and Berlin. His work ranged across analysis, number theory, geometry, mechanics, astronomy, and other branches of mathematics and physics.

Euler had an extraordinary gift for finding connections between problems. Questions about bridges, infinite series, moving bodies, and the properties of numbers all became occasions for mathematical discovery. His books and papers also helped establish much of the notation familiar to students today.

His powers of calculation were matched by a remarkable capacity for explanation. Through his textbooks, he helped make difficult mathematics something that others could learn and carry further. He continued producing mathematics after the loss of his sight, working with assistants who recorded his results.

The Danetian Academy honors Euler as a mathematician and teacher. His place among its honorary academicians reflects a simple conviction: mathematics belongs in Danetian intellectual life, and deserves to be studied, explained, and enjoyed in the language.
