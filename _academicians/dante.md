**Dante Alighieri** (1265–1321) was a Florentine poet and thinker, best known for the *Divine Comedy*. Written in the vernacular, the poem carries its readers through Hell, Purgatory, and Paradise, bringing theology, politics, philosophy, and the lives of ordinary people into a single imagined journey.

Dante also thought carefully about language itself. In *De vulgari eloquentia*, written in Latin, he examined the vernaculars of Italy and sought a literary language capable of reaching beyond a single city. In the *Convivio*, he used the vernacular to discuss philosophical questions with readers who did not know Latin.

His writing helped establish the standing of the Florentine vernacular within Italian literature. He demonstrated its range by putting it to work: in argument, tenderness, anger, humor, and grief.

The Danetian Academy honors Dante for the example he offers to anyone cultivating a literary language. A language finds its strength in what people dare to write in it.
