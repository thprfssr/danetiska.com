**Jacob Grimm** (1785–1863) was a German philologist, lexicographer, and collector of traditional stories. Together with his brother Wilhelm, he published the tales that made their name familiar around the world. His scholarly work was equally far-reaching.

In his *Deutsche Grammatik*, Grimm examined the history of the Germanic languages through their sounds and grammatical forms. Its revised first volume of 1822 set out the consonant correspondences now known as Grimm’s law, building in part on the work of Rasmus Rask. These correspondences helped show that differences between related languages could follow regular historical patterns.

The brothers also began the *Deutsches Wörterbuch*, a historical dictionary whose completion would occupy later generations of scholars. For Jacob Grimm, words, old texts, and inherited stories all offered ways of studying the past.

The Danetian Academy honors Grimm for this breadth of attention. His work speaks to several of its own daily concerns: tracing a sound, understanding an old word, preserving a story, and making a dictionary worth returning to.
