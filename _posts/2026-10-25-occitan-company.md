Visitors to Danetia are sometimes surprised by its surnames. Alongside familiar
surnames such as Petrovos, Georgievos, Theodorovos, and Alexandrovos, there are
surnames which seem more at home in southern France: Carpentier, Molinier,
Sabatier, Faure, Aubanel, Delpont, and Delcastel.

These names are among the most enduring legacies of a brief but important period
of Danetian history: the settlement of the Occitan Company.

## The Genoese Black Sea

During the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, the
[Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) became one of the
great commercial frontiers of the Mediterranean world. The
[Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa)
established trading colonies throughout the region and connected the ports of
the Black Sea with Constantinople and the markets of the Mediterranean.

Danetia, situated between the Danube and the western Black Sea, could hardly
remain outside this commercial system.
[Kustendja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba)
and the surrounding coast
offered access not only to maritime trade, but also to the
[Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube) and the rich
agricultural lands of the interior.

Genoese commerce, however, required protection.
Merchant vessels, warehouses, roads and trading settlements existed in a region
contested by local rulers and threatened periodically by war, piracy and
raiding. Like other medieval commercial powers, the Genoese therefore relied
not only upon their own citizens but upon hired soldiers and sailors drawn from
across the Mediterranean.

It was in this environment that the Occitan Company appeared.

## The Occitan Company

Despite its name, the Occitan Company was not an army of an
[Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitania) state. It
was a mercenary company organized and employed through Genoese commercial
interests and composed principally of men recruited from the
[Occitan-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language)
lands of southern France, together with other Western Europeans.

Its organization belonged to the same Mediterranean military world that
produced the better-known
[Catalan Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Company),
whose soldiers entered Byzantine
service at the beginning of the fourteenth century.

The Genoese brought the Occitan Company eastward to protect their interests on
the western Black Sea. Elements of the company were stationed in and around
Kustendja and at other strategic points along the Danetian coast.

They did not come alone;
soldiers required armorers, farriers, carpenters, merchants, priests, servants
and other craftsmen. Maritime commerce brought sailors and shipwrights. Where
Genoese trade became permanent, families and communities followed. What began
as a military presence consequently developed into a small but significant
Western Romance population along the Danetian coast.

And unlike many medieval mercenary companies, much of this population stayed.

## From Mercenaries to Danetians

The Occitan communities gradually lost their separate identity.

Veterans settled permanently. Merchants acquired property. Craftsmen
established workshops. Their descendants married into the surrounding
population and increasingly spoke Danetian rather than the Romance languages of
their ancestors. Eventually the old language disappeared. But the names did not.

The Western settlement coincided with the period during which hereditary
surnames were becoming increasingly established. As a result, the descendants of
the settlers preserved family names long after they had ceased to regard
themselves as Occitan.

Some preserve occupations:
* Carpentier, the carpenter.
* Faure and Ferrier, associated with smithing and metalworking.
* Molinier, the miller.
* Sabatier, the shoemaker.
* Cavalier and Lancier preserve a more obviously military vocabulary.

Others preserve places, landmarks or descriptions:
Delacosta, Delafont, Delamar, Delariviera, Delcamp, Delcastel, Delmont,
Delmostier and Delpont.

Still others, such as Aubanel and Villanova, survive without appearing
particularly remarkable to modern Danetian ears.

The result is one of the peculiarities of modern Danetian onomastics. A family
named Delcastel may have spoken Danetian for six hundred years. Its members need
have no particular cultural connection with France, just as a Carpentier or
Molinier is today no less Danetian than a Petrovos or Theodorovos.

## A Layer of Danetian History

The Occitan surnames are therefore better understood as part of a broader
Western Romance stratum in Danetian culture.

Not every Western surname necessarily originated with the Company itself. The
Genoese commercial system brought Ligurians, Catalans and speakers of other
Romance languages into contact with Danetia as merchants, sailors and craftsmen.
Some families arrived independently or in later generations.

Nevertheless, Danetian historians traditionally associate the unusually high
concentration of Western Romance surnames with the communities established
during the period of the Occitan Company.

Their survival provides a small reminder of the extraordinary diversity of the
medieval Black Sea.
The Company itself disappeared centuries ago. Its soldiers were absorbed into
the population they had once been hired to defend. Their language disappeared
with them, but their names remained.
