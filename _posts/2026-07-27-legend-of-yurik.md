Yurik Aslan is the national hero of the Danetian people. His life is recounted
in the *Song of Danu*, the Danetian
[national epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_epic).
The chronology of the poem
places his adventures around the 750s CE, during the collapse of the
[Second Turkic Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Turkic_Khaganate),
one of the most turbulent periods in the history of the
[Eurasian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe).

According to the *Song of Danu*, Yurik was orphaned as an infant after a bloody
battle on the winter steppe. Left alone in the snow, he was discovered by a
she-wolf, who suckled him and kept him alive until he was found by a widowed
woman. She adopted the child and raised him as her own.

At the age of seventeen, his family's cattle were stolen during a night raid.
Determined to recover them, he set out in pursuit of the raiders. During the
confrontation, he killed one of the thieves in self-defense. What was meant to
be a brief expedition instead became the beginning of a lifetime of wandering.

He was captured by a tribal lord on charges of murder and of violating the
tribe's territorial authority. Rather than execute him, the tribal lord spared
his life and took him into his service. In this way, Yurik traveled widely
across the steppe and witnessed firsthand the gradual collapse of the Turkic
order.

When the Second Turkic Empire finally imploded, Yurik fled westward along the
great caravan routes of Central Asia. One of the most memorable episodes of the
epic recounts his arrival at a prosperous city in
[Sogdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdia) threatened by an approaching
Turkic army. Yurik instructed the citizens to gather all their valuables into a
single great pile and welcome the invaders as victors, allowing them to depart
unopposed with the treasure. Burdened by their enormous plunder, the invaders
became slow and disorganized. Yurik pursued them, defeated them in battle, and
recovered the city's wealth. But despite the victory, the citizens soon fell
into bitter conflict over the redistribution of the treasure, and Yurik, blamed
for the unrest, was driven into exile.

News of this victory reached the general
[Abu Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muslim),
who recruited Yurik during
the [Abbasid Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_revolution).
After several military campaigns, Yurik once again
departed, eventually crossing the
[Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) into
[Georgia]("https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country)).

It was in Georgia that Yurik first heard of a people living along the
[Don river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_(river)):
five kindred tribes collectively known as the Danetians. Though united by
language and ancestry, the tribes had long been divided by a
[blood feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feud) over
rights to the river's pastures. Every spring, as their herds returned to the
floodplains, old grievances reignited into violence.

Yurik succeeded in reconciling the tribes by establishing a rotational system in
which each tribe shared the most fertile pastures. The *Song of Danu* also
credits him with establishing a code of honor among the tribes.

Following the unification of the five tribes, Yurik was acclaimed King of the
Danetians. His descendants ruled the pastures along the Don river for
generations, and it is here that the epic concludes. The later migration of the
Danetians from the Don to
[Dobrudja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobruja),
following the
[Battle of Levounion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Levounion) in
1091 CE, belongs to a later chapter of Danetian history.

The central concern of the *Song of Danu* is the question of how to live
honorably in a world that rewards cruelty, deception, and violence. For this
reason, it remains one of the most cherished works of Danetian literature and a
defining expression of the nation's identity.
